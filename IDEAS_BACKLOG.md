## Energy and tariff arbitrage

### 1. Bulletproof daytime-dispatch battery hold

**What it does + why the owner cares:** When Octopus schedules a daytime Tesla dispatch, temporarily enable a charge window so the battery does not discharge into the car. This targets the known 30p–£1.50 opportunity, with an explicit restore transaction after the dispatch.

**Entities and data needed:** `binary_sensor.octopus_energy_00000000_0000_0000_0000_000000000000_intelligent_dispatching`, `sensor.octopus_energy_00000000_0000_0000_0000_000000000000_intelligent_state`, dispatch-plan attributes, `sensor.solis_inverter_charge_window`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_charge_current_limit`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, and `sensor.solis_inverter_timed_charging`. Uses `control.py` writes to charge-window registers `43143–43146`, with readback and guaranteed restoration.

**Rough value:** 30p–£1.50 per daytime dispatch.

**Effort:** New guarded automation or CLI wrapper with persistent pre-change state, timeout, readback, and recovery handling.

**Risk or catch:** This is only safe if restore failure leaves the system in a known state. A forgotten active window would import at peak rate. Count the two state changes per dispatch against assumed 10k-write endurance. It must never use the removed network/browser write path.

### 2. DST-safe charge-window and inverter-clock guardian

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Detects when the inverter’s timezone-naive clock no longer matches UK tariff time, especially on 2026-10-25, and alerts before the six-hour cheap period shifts to 22:30–04:30 GMT.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_charge_window`, `sensor.solis_inverter_timed_charging`, `binary_sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_off_peak`, current local time, and read-only values from inverter RTC registers `43000–43005` via `control.py show`. No HA RTC entity currently exists; adding one requires a read-only integration sensor while respecting the one-Modbus-session limit.

**Rough value:** Potentially £0.30–£1+ per affected night, plus prevents shifted daily-counter accounting.

**Effort:** Read-only diagnostic sensor and notification; manual `control.py set-time --apply` for correction.

**Risk or catch:** Do not automate clock writes without explicit opt-in and readback. Check after every HA/inverter restart and at both seasonal transitions.

### 3. SOC- and forecast-aware 43141 decision card

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Calculates the lowest sensible overnight charge current for that particular night, while preserving the fixed 23:30–05:30 window. It considers SOC at window opening, the real window length, battery capacity, and tomorrow’s forecast; it recommends 50 A when the pack needs a full charge and avoids needless changes when it is already nearly full.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_voltage`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_charge_current_limit`, `sensor.solis_inverter_charge_window`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_tomorrow`, `sensor.solis_inverter_tomorrow_verdict`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_today`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_rate`, and `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_standing_charge`. Applying a recommendation requires one direct CLI write to register `43141`, with readback.

**Rough value:** Usually 0–10p/day; larger only on unusually high-SOC or very poor-solar days.

**Effort:** Template/calculation sensor and dashboard card; optional once-per-night guarded CLI automation.

**Risk or catch:** The owner’s 50 A setting is already deliberate, and the settled strategy must not be reopened. Use hysteresis and do not write merely because the forecast changes. Never implement this as a SOC taper through `43117`.

### 4. Intelligent-dispatch flexibility score for the air conditioner

**What it does + why the owner cares:** During an Octopus daytime car dispatch, identifies whether running or pre-cooling the living room is cheaper than running the same cooling load at peak rate. It makes the dispatch’s extended cheap billing visible and can optionally trigger a bounded comfort automation.

**Entities and data needed:** `binary_sensor.octopus_energy_00000000_0000_0000_0000_000000000000_intelligent_dispatching`, dispatch-plan attributes on the Octopus Tesla entity, `climate.living_room_air_conditioner`, `switch.living_room_air_conditioner_power`, `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`, `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_temperature`, `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_humidity`, and `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_rate`. The exact dispatch schedule must be confirmed in entity attributes.

**Rough value:** A few pence per event; potentially 10–30p/day on heavy cooling days.

**Effort:** Automation plus temperature guard; new thermal model is optional.

**Risk or catch:** Comfort and humidity matter more than the small saving. Do not run the AC merely because a dispatch exists. A daytime dispatch can also expose the battery to car load, so this should be paired with the hold protocol above rather than silently assuming battery energy is free.

### 5. Cheap-window load-shift audit with measured payback

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Separates controllable loads that are genuinely movable into 23:30–05:30 from loads that are already there, then reports the realised saving rather than assuming every night load is worth shifting.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_consumption_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_yesterday`, `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`, `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`, `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_yesterday`, `sensor.rsdose4_dosing_queue`, `sensor.rsmat_<serial>_daily_average_usage`, `time.rsmat_<serial>_schedule_time`, and the Octopus off-peak entity `binary_sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_off_peak`.

**Rough value:** Insight first; likely only a few pence/day because the aquarium equipment has no measured electrical submeter and the water-change mat is already scheduled at 00:00.

**Effort:** Template sensors and a daily dashboard/report.

**Risk or catch:** No aquarium or water-mat power/energy entities exist, so their actual tariff value cannot currently be proved. Do not move dosing or water changes purely for tariff reasons without confirming biological/process constraints.

### 6. Standing-charge-aware daily settlement

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Shows the true daily electricity position as peak import, cheap import, export credit, and standing charge, preventing the owner from treating a low-import day as free.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_rate`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_standing_charge`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_previous_accumulative_cost`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_previous_accumulative_consumption`, `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_import_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_export_today`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<export-mpan>_export_current_rate`, and the current/next/previous day-rate event attributes.

**Rough value:** Insight only; typically 54.46p/day of electricity standing charge, plus accurate attribution of variable cost.

**Effort:** Template sensor or small tariff-accounting integration.

**Risk or catch:** Do not use the Octopus `previous_accumulative_*` entities as HA Energy sources; they have incorrect day-boundary behaviour. Use them only as reconciliation data, with Solis daily counters and rate timestamps as the operational source.

### 7. Gas-versus-electric break-even calculator

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Calculates when electricity is cheaper than gas for a genuine thermal load, including the electricity standing charge only at the whole-day level. This prevents misleading comparisons between the 7.78p/kWh gas unit rate and electricity’s 6.9p cheap or 30.46p peak rate.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.octopus_energy_gas_<gas-meter>_<mprn>_current_rate`, `sensor.octopus_energy_gas_<gas-meter>_<mprn>_current_standing_charge`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_rate`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_standing_charge`, and `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`. A boiler-efficiency value and an electric-heating COP/thermal-output measurement are missing.

**Rough value:** Insight only until heating measurements exist.

**Effort:** Simple calculator card; meaningful automation needs new heating/COP measurements.

**Risk or catch:** The inventory contains only a cooling AC entity, not electric heating or boiler-output sensors. Do not infer that the AC can economically replace gas heating, and do not automate a gas/electric switchover from unit prices alone.

## Solar forecast and prediction

### 1. Add a separately calibrated peak-power forecast

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Predicts hourly instantaneous AC output and the time of the daily peak, correcting the known ~20% low bias without corrupting the daily-energy forecast. Useful for spotting clipping, estimating export bursts, and deciding whether a daytime Tesla dispatch can be supplied by solar.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_sw_string`, and `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_se_string`; forecast irradiance from the existing five models and both `PLANES`. For an honest AC model, add a read-only sensor for inverter AC output from registers `33079`/`33080`; the current total solar sensor is DC-side. Record daily/hourly observed maxima and fit a peak or high-quantile model separately from `EFFECTIVE_KWP`.

**Rough value:** Insight only, with occasional money/safety value from identifying clipping or a usable solar surplus.

**Effort:** New integration sensor plus forecast-code and Recorder-history work.

**Risk or catch:** Never rescale the existing energy-fitted hourly line. DC PV power and AC output are not interchangeable because of conversion losses.

### 2. Publish independent SW and SE forecasts

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Produces separate hourly and daily forecasts for the 12-panel SW roof and 8-panel SE roof, showing when each roof should contribute. This makes the forecast useful for diagnosing a weak string and for predicting early-day versus late-day generation.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_sw_string` and `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_se_string`; the verified geometry of 12 × 400 W SW at 207°/21° and 8 × 400 W SE at 117°/24°; the five Open-Meteo model outputs. Add per-plane forecast attributes or sensors. Per-plane energy counters do not exist, so validation must initially use integrated power readings rather than inverter daily counters.

**Rough value:** Insight only, potentially valuable for detecting a failed string before total generation looks abnormal.

**Effort:** Forecast-code changes and two to four new read-only forecast entities/cards.

**Risk or catch:** The SW/SE string assignment is explicitly inferred from the current mapping, not cable-traced. Keep the mapping easy to correct.

### 3. Track forecast error by issue time and horizon

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Stores what the forecast said for today/tomorrow when it was issued, then compares it with actual generation. It can report bias, RMSE, error by forecast horizon, and whether the model is becoming optimistic or pessimistic.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_tomorrow`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_yesterday`, and `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_today`. Extend the existing `solar_actuals.json` workflow so each actual is paired with forecast snapshots, rather than recording only the final actual. The existing `solar_forecast.py calibrate` process can then use the expanded dataset.

**Rough value:** Insight only; prevents false confidence and improves future calibration.

**Effort:** New persisted forecast-history format and a small dashboard/report.

**Risk or catch:** Today’s forecast is a moving target. Store the issue timestamp and target date, otherwise the error calculation will compare an end-of-day revision with an earlier actual expectation.

### 4. Add ensemble spread and forecast confidence

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Exposes not just the five-model mean but their disagreement, with labels such as stable, mixed, or highly uncertain. A 30 kWh mean with tight agreement is materially different from 30 kWh spanning 20–40 kWh.

**Entities and data needed:** The five existing model series—`ecmwf_ifs025`, `gfs_seamless`, `icon_seamless`, `ukmo_seamless`, and `meteofrance_seamless`—from `solar_forecast.py` / the vendored forecast module. Add daily and hourly spread attributes to `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_tomorrow` or new forecast sensors. Existing `sensor.solis_inverter_tomorrow_verdict` can incorporate the confidence label.

**Rough value:** Insight only, but directly improves the honesty of the low/borderline/high decision.

**Effort:** Template-style forecast output change and one dashboard card.

**Risk or catch:** Spread is model disagreement, not a guaranteed prediction interval. Keep the existing measured RMSE-based uncertainty rather than presenting spread as statistical certainty.

### 5. Detect clouds, shading, and abnormal string behaviour in real time

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Compares the expected clear-sky/weather-adjusted SW and SE curves with live output and identifies “cloud event”, “one plane underperforming”, “both planes suppressed”, or “inverter/telemetry suspect”. This turns the forecast into an operational diagnostic rather than a decorative line.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_sw_string`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_se_string`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load`, and `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power`; per-plane forecast curves from idea 2; `sun.sun`, `sensor.sun_next_rising`, and `sensor.sun_next_setting` for daylight gating. A rolling residual over several samples is needed to avoid reacting to the 10-second polling noise.

**Rough value:** Insight and fault detection; could identify a shaded or failed string early.

**Effort:** New derived sensors and alert/dashboard logic.

**Risk or catch:** A weather forecast residual alone cannot distinguish clouds from local shading. Confidence improves substantially with per-plane curves and, ideally, a roof-mounted irradiance sensor; none currently exists.

### 6. Estimate seasonal soiling and degradation separately from weather error

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Tracks the long-term clear-sky-normalised performance of each plane and flags a persistent decline that could indicate dirt, snow, vegetation shading, connector trouble, or degradation. It avoids treating one cloudy day as panel degradation.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing SW/SE power sensors, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_yesterday`, the five-model irradiance history, both plane geometries, and `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_health` as contextual battery information. A rolling 14–30 day residual baseline is needed; no soiling sensor exists.

**Rough value:** Usually insight only; a real fault or cleaning opportunity could recover several percent of seasonal yield.

**Effort:** New historical analysis and dashboard trend, without hardware.

**Risk or catch:** Short history and weather-model error make this weak initially. It should require repeated clear or mostly clear daylight evidence and must not silently alter `EFFECTIVE_KWP`; recalibration and degradation diagnosis are separate.

### 7. Extend the ensemble to a seven-day outlook with battery relevance

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Shows daily generation, confidence, and likely battery-refill status for the next seven days, not only today and tomorrow. It helps the owner see whether a run of dull weather is coming before relying on solar to cover daytime demand.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing five-model Open-Meteo approach, both `PLANES`, and new `forecast_days=7` retrieval/caching. Context sensors: `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_health`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_yesterday`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_consumption_today`, and the fixed `sensor.solis_inverter_charge_window`. Add seven daily forecast values and a probability/band for crossing the existing 25 kWh threshold.

**Rough value:** Insight only, with possible avoided peak import if used for manual planning.

**Effort:** Forecast API/cache expansion and a compact seven-day card.

**Risk or catch:** Accuracy will degrade with horizon. The current ±2 kWh uncertainty is based on short-horizon observed RMSE and must not be reused unchanged for days 4–7.

### 8. Derive a “solar opportunity window” sensor

**What it does / why the owner cares:** Reports the next period when expected solar surplus should exceed house demand and battery headroom is available—for example, “likely surplus 11:00–15:00, ~X kWh available”. This makes the forecast actionable for manually timing a daytime Tesla dispatch or another flexible load, without changing inverter settings.

**Entities and data needed:** Forecast hourly output; `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load` and its history; `sensor.solis_inverter_house_yesterday`; `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power`, and `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_health`; `sensor.tesla_battery`, `sensor.tesla_charging_state`, `binary_sensor.tesla_plugged_in`, and `sensor.tesla_charger_power`. A house-load baseline and an explicit ~5 kW battery-delivery limit are required.

**Rough value:** Potentially tens of pence to £1.50 per suitable Tesla-dispatch occurrence, but only when the owner manually acts or separately opts into a guarded automation.

**Effort:** Derived-sensor/template logic plus a dashboard card.

**Risk or catch:** It must remain advisory until the parked daytime-dispatch restore sequence is bulletproof. Do not add a network inverter write path or alter the fixed 23:30–05:30 charge window.

## Battery health and inverter diagnostics

### Battery throughput, cycle and depth-of-discharge ledger

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Tracks daily charge/discharge throughput, equivalent full cycles, and a histogram of SOC swing depth. This turns the three-pack’s longevity into measurable evidence instead of relying on the static 90% SOH reading.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_charge_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_discharge_today`, plus Recorder history. Use the inverter’s 10-second telemetry where available; daily counters are only 0.1 kWh resolution and reset at midnight.

**Rough value:** Insight only; useful for warranty and replacement decisions.

**Effort:** New statistics/template integration and dashboard card.

**Risk or catch:** History only recently began, so meaningful cycle distributions require months of data. Equivalent cycles are estimates, not manufacturer cycle counts.

### Usable-capacity and fade tracker

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Estimates usable pack capacity from SOC change against integrated battery power during sustained charge/discharge periods, then plots the estimate against cumulative throughput. It can show whether the nominal ~15.4 kWh pack is gradually losing capacity.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_voltage`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_current`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_health`; Recorder history. Best results come from complete excursions toward the deliberate 10% floor, not shallow daily fluctuations.

**Rough value:** Insight only, with potential early replacement warning.

**Effort:** New integration code or external Recorder analysis.

**Risk or catch:** SOC quantisation, conversion losses, standby consumption, and the BMS’s rounded 90% SOH make this a trend estimator, not a certified capacity test.

### Aggregate internal-resistance proxy

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Measures voltage sag during comparable discharge-current steps, calculating a repeatable pack-level ΔV/ΔI proxy. Rising resistance can identify worsening battery behaviour before capacity visibly collapses.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_voltage`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_current`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, and `binary_sensor.solis_inverter_battery_charging`; 10-second Recorder history.

**Rough value:** Insight and early warning.

**Effort:** New time-series analysis and a diagnostic plot/alert.

**Risk or catch:** This measures all three parallel LV5200 modules together; it cannot identify which module is responsible. Temperature and SOC must be used to compare like with like.

### Battery behaviour anomaly sentinel

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Flags combinations such as SOC falling unusually quickly, charge power collapsing while the charge limit remains 50 A, unexplained power cutbacks, repeated abrupt voltage drops, or SOH falling independently of throughput. It provides a practical “investigate the pack” warning.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_battery`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_health`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_voltage`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_current`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_charge_current_limit`, and `binary_sensor.solis_inverter_battery_charging`.

**Rough value:** Safety/trust insight; potentially valuable before a module failure becomes obvious.

**Effort:** Template/statistics sensors plus guarded HA notifications.

**Risk or catch:** It detects pack-level symptoms only. A failing module cannot be isolated without per-module BMS telemetry.

### DC-to-AC conversion-efficiency curve

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Calculates inverter efficiency across operating power, showing whether losses remain normal at low load, near the 5 kW battery limit, and near the 6 kW inverter ceiling.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power` for DC PV input, plus a new read-only sensor for AC output from verified registers `33079`/`33080`. `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power` is also needed when assessing battery-to-AC operation; the current integration does not expose AC output.

**Rough value:** Insight only; can reveal inverter degradation or abnormal standby losses.

**Effort:** Extend the existing single Solarman sweep and add one HA sensor and plot.

**Risk or catch:** Do not derive AC output from house load and battery/PV values; those are on different electrical sides and create phantom efficiency.

### Clipping and string-underperformance detector

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Separates genuine inverter clipping from a weak roof/string. It would detect AC output flattening near 6 kW while DC PV continues rising, and compare the SW and SE string shapes against their different nameplates.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_sw_string`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_se_string`, plus a new read-only AC-output sensor from registers `33079`/`33080`. Historical sun position and cloud context would improve confidence.

**Rough value:** Insight only; helps distinguish expected clipping from a panel, cable, or MPPT problem.

**Effort:** New diagnostic sensors and dashboard analysis.

**Risk or catch:** The SW/SE register assignment remains inferred, not cable-traced. The existing forecast’s clipping estimate is not a measurement and should not be reused as proof.

### Grid-quality health score

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Produces rolling voltage percentiles, excursion duration, and a simple healthy/borderline status from the existing inverter grid-voltage stream. It would turn the current chart into an ongoing operational check.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_voltage`; optionally a new read-only frequency sensor from verified register `33094` for a fuller score. Use HA Recorder statistics, not a second Modbus poller.

**Rough value:** Safety/trust insight.

**Effort:** Template/statistics sensors and an alert card.

**Risk or catch:** This differs from the rejected standalone voltage logger: it reuses the existing HA telemetry and does not revive the archive, launchd process, or DNO gap-analysis workflow. Frequency is currently missing from the inventory.

### Battery temperature-aware diagnostics

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Correlates charge acceptance, voltage sag, SOH changes, and power limits with battery temperature, helping distinguish thermal derating from electrical ageing.

**Entities and data needed:** No battery-temperature entity exists in the inventory. It requires a verified Fox BMS temperature register exposed through the existing read-only sweep, or separately installed temperature hardware.

**Rough value:** Insight and safety; especially useful for explaining seasonal or cold-weather performance.

**Effort:** New register mapping if available; otherwise new hardware and integration work.

**Risk or catch:** Do not infer battery temperature from voltage, current, or room temperature. Without an actual pack-temperature measurement, this idea cannot be implemented honestly.

## Tesla and EV

### 1. Per-session Tesla cost and energy ledger

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Splits every home charge into start/end, pack energy, AC energy, charger loss, tariff cost, and effective pence per delivered kWh. This makes the real cost of each discrete Octopus dispatch visible; the existing verified example was 8.384 kWh AC for 7.88 kWh into the pack, about 6% loss.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.tesla_home_charging_energy`, `sensor.tesla_charge_energy_added`, `sensor.tesla_charger_power`, `sensor.tesla_charger_voltage`, `sensor.tesla_charger_actual_current`, `sensor.tesla_charging_state`, `binary_sensor.tesla_plugged_in`, and Recorder timestamps. Attribute cost using `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_rate` plus historical Octopus rate data. Use the existing 180–280 V and current-greater-than-zero home-charge gate.

**Rough value:** Insight plus typically about £2 per 8 kWh session when a charge would otherwise land at peak rate.

**Effort:** New statistics/template integration and dashboard card.

**Risk or catch:** `sensor.tesla_home_charging_energy` has no pre-2026-08-27 history. AC energy is intentionally higher than Tesla app pack energy because it includes onboard-charger losses.

### 2. Rolling onboard-charger efficiency monitor

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Tracks AC-to-pack efficiency per session and over time, showing whether the roughly 94% observed efficiency is stable and flagging unusual sessions or possible charger/cabling problems.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.tesla_home_charging_energy`, `sensor.tesla_charge_energy_added`, `sensor.tesla_charger_voltage`, `sensor.tesla_charger_actual_current`, and session boundaries from `sensor.tesla_charging_state`/`binary_sensor.tesla_plugged_in`.

**Rough value:** Insight only, with potential early fault detection.

**Effort:** Template/statistics sensors and a trend card.

**Risk or catch:** Efficiency varies with charge rate, temperature, and session size. A short or interrupted session is too noisy to diagnose from alone.

### 3. Planned-versus-actual Octopus dispatch audit

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Compares each planned Intelligent dispatch with what actually happened: scheduled time versus observed charging time, planned kWh versus measured AC and pack kWh, and completed versus missed/under-delivered slots.

**Entities and data needed:** Existing read-only `octopus.py` data from `flexPlannedDispatches` and `completedDispatches`; `binary_sensor.octopus_energy_00000000_0000_0000_0000_000000000000_intelligent_dispatching`; `sensor.octopus_energy_00000000_0000_0000_0000_000000000000_intelligent_state`; `sensor.tesla_charging_state`; `sensor.tesla_home_charging_energy`; `sensor.tesla_charge_energy_added`; and Recorder history. The existing `/api/state` car payload already contains planned spans and kWh estimates.

**Rough value:** Insight and reliability; prevents discovering a missed ready-by charge after the fact.

**Effort:** New historical ledger/API fields and a dashboard or notification.

**Risk or catch:** Octopus dispatch-state meanings are community/inferred rather than fully documented; retain raw states alongside friendly labels.

### 4. Daytime-dispatch battery-drain observer

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Detects an Octopus dispatch outside the fixed 23:30–05:30 window and records whether the house battery supplied the car instead of cheap grid energy. It quantifies the parked opportunity without changing inverter settings.

**Entities and data needed:** `binary_sensor.octopus_energy_00000000_0000_0000_0000_000000000000_intelligent_dispatching`; existing `/api/state` planned/current dispatch data; `sensor.tesla_charger_power`; `sensor.tesla_home_charging_power`; `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power`; `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_power`; `sensor.solis_inverter_charge_window`; and `binary_sensor.solis_inverter_timed_charging`. Use Octopus dispatch timing rather than the rejected GPS home-gate approach.

**Rough value:** Insight first; each qualifying event may expose roughly £0.30–£1.50 of avoidable value, depending on duration and export/battery conditions.

**Effort:** Template/Recorder analysis and an observational dashboard or alert.

**Risk or catch:** It must not force a charge window or write registers. Octopus state can lag, and the Tesla voltage/current gate still cannot independently prove physical location.

### 5. Read-only flexible-load signal for the house

**What it does + why the owner cares:** Produces a clean “car is drawing approximately 7 kW now” signal and shows whether that load is coincident with battery discharge, grid import, or solar export. This helps interpret house-load spikes and validates the Sankey’s Tesla branch.

**Entities and data needed:** `sensor.tesla_home_charging_power`, `sensor.tesla_charger_power`, `sensor.tesla_charger_actual_current`, `sensor.tesla_charger_voltage`, `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load`, `sensor.solis_inverter_battery_power`, and `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_power`.

**Rough value:** Insight only, with useful operational visibility.

**Effort:** Template sensors plus a time-series card.

## Anomaly detection and alerting

### 1. Inverter/logger freshness watchdog

- **Effort:** Template sensor and automation.
- **Risk or catch:** Require SOC below 90%, off-peak/timed charging active, at least 20 minutes since the window began, and battery power below 200 W for 20 minutes. Suppress it at high SOC because a small overnight charge is normal when solar left the pack nearly full.

### 4. Solar production below the calibrated forecast

- **What it does** and why the owner would care: Flags a dead string, inverter/PV fault, or severe underperformance while there is still time to investigate.
- **Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_sw_string`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_se_string`, `sun.sun`, `sensor.sun_next_rising`, and `sensor.sun_next_setting`. The existing forecast coordinator’s hourly `wh_hours` must be exposed as an expected cumulative-energy sensor; the daily total alone cannot provide a time-correct comparison.
- **Rough value:** Potentially material avoided generation loss; otherwise insight only.
- **Effort:** New integration/template sensor using the already-built five-model hourly forecast.
- **Risk or catch:** Alert only when expected cumulative production exceeds 3 kWh and actual cumulative production is below 65% of expectation for two consecutive hourly checks. Require a separate string alarm when one string is below 50% of its learned clear-weather share while the other is producing; never treat low irradiance or night as a fault.

### 5. Overnight unexplained load left on

- **What it does** and why the owner would care: Finds a heater, pump, appliance, or other load running continuously when the house should be quiet.
- **Entities and data needed:** `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load`, `switch.living_room_air_conditioner_power`, `sensor.tesla_charger_power`, and optionally `climate.living_room_air_conditioner`.
- **Rough value:** Usually pence to tens of pence per occurrence; useful fault insight.
- **Effort:** Statistics helper plus automation.
- **Risk or catch:** Learn a 14-day 01:00-05:00 baseline, then alert when the car is not charging, AC is off, and load is both above 1,000 W and 500 W above baseline for 30 minutes. There is no fridge or immersion-specific sensor, so attribution needs a dedicated metered plug or circuit sensor.

### 6. Aquarium dosing missed-cycle watchdog

- **What it does** and why the owner would care: Detects a dosing pump that is online but has stopped dosing, without paging for intentionally disabled heads.
- **Entities and data needed:** For active heads 1 and 2: `sensor.rsdose4_head_1_status`, `sensor.rsdose4_head_1_doses_today`, `sensor.rsdose4_head_1_daily_doses`, `sensor.rsdose4_head_1_auto_dosed_today`, `switch.rsdose4_head_1_schedule_enabled`, and corresponding head-2 entities; `binary_sensor.rsdose4_cloud_connection_state`; `number.rsdose4_stock_alert_days`; `sensor.rsdose4_head_1_remaining_days` and head 2 equivalent.
- **Rough value:** High aquarium-safety value; chemical imbalance can become expensive and harmful.
- **Effort:** Statistics/history-based automation.
- **Risk or catch:** Alert after two missed expected hourly cycles—about 2 hours 20 minutes—with schedule enabled and status not healthy. Alert stock at `remaining_days <= 10`, with one notification per head until refilled. Exclude heads 3 and 4 because their `not-setup` state and disabled schedules are intentional.

### 7. Water-change mat fault and roll exhaustion alert

- **What it does** and why the owner would care: Warns about a disconnected/faulted mat or a roll that will run out before the next maintenance cycle.
- **Entities and data needed:** `binary_sensor.rsmat_<serial>_sensor_state`, `binary_sensor.rsmat_<serial>_cloud_connection_state`, `binary_sensor.rsmat_<serial>_is_ec_sensor_connected`, `sensor.rsmat_<serial>_days_till_end_of_roll`, `sensor.rsmat_<serial>_remaining_length`, `sensor.rsmat_<serial>_today_usage`, `switch.rsmat_<serial>_auto_advance`, and `time.rsmat_<serial>_schedule_time`.
- **Rough value:** High aquarium-safety and maintenance value.
- **Effort:** Automation and a small dashboard diagnostic.
- **Risk or catch:** Alert `sensor_state` problem after five minutes immediately; alert remaining roll at two days, then once at one day. Treat cloud loss separately after 15 minutes because the local device may still operate; detect a failed scheduled advance only if remaining length is unchanged through the next 24-hour cycle.

### 8. Network degradation affecting the aquarium and inverter path

- **What it does** and why the owner would care: Separates a real connectivity failure from a merely stale cloud integration before aquarium or energy alarms cascade.
- **Entities and data needed:** `sensor.rsdose4_wifi_signal_strength`, `sensor.rsdose4_wifi_signal_quality`, `binary_sensor.rsdose4_cloud_connection_state`, `sensor.rsmat_<serial>_wifi_signal_strength`, `sensor.rsmat_<serial>_wifi_signal_quality`, `binary_sensor.rsmat_<serial>_cloud_connection_state`, `binary_sensor.mesh_wi_fi_wan_status`, and `binary_sensor.nova_wan_status`.
- **Rough value:** Insight only, but reduces false diagnosis and delayed fault response.
- **Effort:** Template sensors and grouped alerting.
- **Risk or catch:** Warn at RSSI below -70 dBm for 15 minutes or quality `poor`; critical only after cloud connectivity is off for 30 minutes. The mesh WAN entities are already `unavailable`, so do not alert on their current state; alert only on a transition from a previously valid state, or after they have been re-baselined.

### 9. Grid-voltage limit watchdog

- **What it does** and why the owner would care: Alerts to sustained high or low supply voltage before inverter protection or nuisance trips become the visible symptom.
- **Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_voltage`; use the established UK limits of 216.2 V and 253.0 V.
- **Rough value:** Safety and equipment-protection insight.
- **Effort:** Numeric-state automation and notification cooldown.
- **Risk or catch:** Warn below 218 V or above 251 V for 10 minutes; escalate when crossing 216.2/253.0 V for two minutes. Add hysteresis of 1 V and suppress repeats for six hours. This is read-only monitoring; never alter the protected DNO registers.

## Data science on the recorder history

### 1. Validate the “rest of house” energy remainder

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** For each complete day, compare the Sankey’s derived rest-of-house consumption with the inverter’s independent house-consumption counter. This resolves the currently open 1.3 kWh discrepancy and tells the owner whether the dashboard breakdown is trustworthy.
- **Entities and data needed:** Recorder history for `sensor.solis_inverter_house_consumption_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_yesterday`, `sensor.tesla_home_charging_energy`, and `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`; daily `change` statistics and the Energy dashboard date range. Needs at least several complete days after `sensor.solis_inverter_house_yesterday` became available.
- **Rough value:** Insight only, but it determines whether the house-load breakdown can be trusted.
- **Effort:** Small one-off SQL/notebook analysis or dashboard diagnostic card.
- **Risk or catch:** The inverter instantaneous `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load` and its energy counter are already known to measure different paths; do not treat their integration as ground truth.

### 2. Disaggregate Tesla, AC, and residual load signatures

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** Use the 10-second `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load` series to identify step changes and sustained signatures for Tesla charging, air conditioning, and everything else. Quantify when the residual load rises, how long events last, and whether the current “rest of house” is hiding a meaningful appliance.
- **Entities and data needed:** Recorder history for `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load`, `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`, `sensor.tesla_charger_actual_current`, `sensor.tesla_charger_voltage`, `sensor.tesla_charger_power`, `sensor.tesla_home_charging_power`, `sensor.tesla_charging_state`, `binary_sensor.tesla_plugged_in`, `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`, and `switch.living_room_air_conditioner_power`; ideally 4–8 weeks of 10-second data.
- **Rough value:** Insight only, potentially identifying avoidable loads worth tens of pence per day.
- **Effort:** Moderate analysis code and event labelling; no new hardware for Tesla/AC, but exact appliance names require plug submeters.
- **Risk or catch:** Tesla dispatch is discrete and AC energy updates in hourly lumps, so raw state transitions must be treated as labels rather than perfectly synchronized measurements. This differs from the rejected GPS home-gate idea: it analyses recorder signatures and does not change charging detection.

### 3. Quantify the true overnight phantom load

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** Estimate the house’s always-on baseline during carefully selected intervals when Tesla charging, AC operation, and obvious activity are absent. Report median, percentile, and seasonal drift rather than a single guessed wattage.
- **Entities and data needed:** 10-second Recorder history for `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load` and `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`; exclusion masks from `sensor.tesla_charger_actual_current`, `sensor.tesla_charger_voltage`, `sensor.tesla_charger_power`, `sensor.tesla_charging_state`, `switch.living_room_air_conditioner_power`, `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`, and `light.bedroom_lights`, `light.study`; ideally 30+ nights.
- **Rough value:** Usually an insight, with approximately 1 kWh/day worth about 7–30p/day depending on what can be switched off.
- **Effort:** Moderate SQL/windowing analysis; a template sensor could expose the rolling baseline afterward.
- **Risk or catch:** “Everything else off” cannot be proven from the current inventory. Treat the result as a statistical baseline, not appliance attribution.

### 4. Cluster day types and infer occupancy patterns

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** Cluster days by half-hour load shape into working-day, home-day, Tesla-dispatch, hot-weather, and unusual-event profiles. Cross-check clusters against lights, phone presence, Tesla activity, AC state, and Octopus dispatch to reveal when the house is occupied or behaving unusually.
- **Entities and data needed:** Recorder history for `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load`, `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`, `light.bedroom1`, `light.bedroom2`, `light.bedroom3`, `light.bedroom_lights`, `light.study`, `light.study1_light`, `light.study2_light_2`, `light.study3_light_3`, `device_tracker.<phone_2>`, `device_tracker.<phone_1>`, `binary_sensor.tesla_user_present`, `binary_sensor.tesla_plugged_in`, `sensor.tesla_charging_state`, and `climate.living_room_air_conditioner`; needs several weeks of history.
- **Rough value:** Insight only, potentially useful for detecting abnormal consumption or improving future simulations.
- **Effort:** Moderate notebook/SQL analysis plus optional dashboard view.
- **Risk or catch:** `device_tracker.<phone_1>` is currently unknown and `binary_sensor.tesla_user_present` is not a reliable whole-house occupancy label. Clusters should be named “load patterns,” with occupancy treated as inferred.

### 5. Separate weather-driven AC demand from background demand

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** Regress daily and hourly load against indoor/outdoor temperature and humidity to estimate how much extra consumption hot or humid weather causes, and whether AC operation is producing measurable comfort benefit.
- **Entities and data needed:** Recorder history for `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_temperature`, `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_humidity`, `sensor.lumi_living_room_temperature`, `sensor.lumi_living_room_humidity`, `climate.living_room_air_conditioner`, `switch.living_room_air_conditioner_power`, and `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`; load comparison from `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load` and `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`. Needs a warm-weather sample spanning multiple weeks.
- **Rough value:** Insight plus possible AC savings, commonly several pence per hot day; it can quantify whether comfort settings are worth the energy.
- **Effort:** Moderate regression and lag analysis.
- **Risk or catch:** `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_temperature` is the available outdoor-like probe, but its placement and representativeness must be verified. There is no separate AC live-power sensor; its energy entity updates in hourly lumps.

### 6. Build degree-day-normalised consumption

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** Construct cooling degree days from `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_temperature`, then compare daily house consumption after removing Tesla charging and estimated AC energy. This gives a fair baseline for comparing unusually hot periods and detecting genuine changes in background demand.
- **Entities and data needed:** Daily Recorder history for `sensor.solis_inverter_house_consumption_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_yesterday`, `sensor.tesla_home_charging_energy`, `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`, `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_temperature`, plus `sensor.solis_inverter_grid_import_today` and `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_today` for context.
- **Rough value:** Insight only initially; useful for deciding whether a future efficiency change actually worked.
- **Effort:** Moderate analysis, with a small amount of calibration to choose the indoor comfort base temperature.
- **Risk or catch:** The recorder has only just begun accumulating reliable inverter history, so this needs at least 6–12 weeks and is more useful after summer data accumulates. Heating fuel use is not an electricity-load substitute.

### 7. Detect seasonal or equipment-change shifts in the residual baseline

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** Apply change-point detection to overnight load, daily residual load, AC energy, and Tesla-excluded consumption to identify a fridge/network/aquarium change or a seasonal shift that ordinary daily totals obscure.
- **Entities and data needed:** Long-term Recorder history for `sensor.solis_inverter_house_load`, `sensor.house_load_excluding_car`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_consumption_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_house_yesterday`, `sensor.tesla_home_charging_energy`, `sensor.living_room_air_conditioner_energy_today`, `sensor.router_power`, `sensor.rsdose4_head_1_daily_dose`, `sensor.rsdose4_head_2_daily_dose`, `sensor.rsmat_<serial>_today_usage`, and the smart-plug power entities if they become available: `sensor.tz3000_cehuw1lw_ts011f_power`, `sensor.tz3000_okaz9tjs_ts011f_power`, and `sensor.antenna_plug_power`.
- **Rough value:** Insight only; it could uncover a persistent 10–100 W load before it becomes an unexplained bill increase.
- **Effort:** Moderate statistical analysis and event annotation.
- **Risk or catch:** The three smart-plug power sensors are currently unavailable, so aquarium/network attribution is limited to total-load change points and device activity metadata.

### 8. Measure forecast and string-performance bias by weather regime

- **What it does + why the owner cares:** Compare forecast versus measured solar by day and by morning/afternoon regime, then test whether errors track cloud conditions, temperature, or the SW/SE roof split. This improves confidence in borderline battery and tariff decisions without replacing the settled five-model ensemble.
- **Entities and data needed:** Recorder history for `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_yesterday`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_sw_string`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_power_se_string`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_today`, `sensor.solis_inverter_solar_forecast_tomorrow`, `sensor.solis_inverter_tomorrow_weather`, `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_temperature`, and `sensor.lumi_lumi_weather_humidity`; needs a materially longer sample than the current few recorded days.
- **Rough value:** Insight only, with possible indirect value from avoiding a few poor battery/EV decisions per month.
- **Effort:** Moderate analysis and a bias-report card.
- **Risk or catch:** Daily forecast totals are the calibrated contract; hourly peaks are deliberately energy-fitted and read low. The analysis should report bias and uncertainty, not rescale the hourly forecast or revert to a single weather model.

## Household operations and delight

### DST charge-window guard

- **What it does / why the owner cares:** On each UK daylight-saving transition, sends a prominent reminder to verify the inverter clock and, if needed, manually run `control.py set-time --apply`, preventing one hour of expensive charging and a missed cheap hour.
- **Entities and data needed:** `sensor.solis_inverter_charge_window`, `binary_sensor.solis_inverter_timed_charging`, `sensor.octopus_energy_electricity_<meter>_<mpan>_current_rate`, calendar/date trigger, and the inverter RTC at registers `43000-43005` (not currently exposed as an HA entity).
- **Rough value:** Potentially £0.30–£1 per transition, plus correct daily-counter alignment.
- **Effort:** Automation and notification; optionally add a read-only RTC sensor.
- **Risk or catch:** Must remain a reminder/manual CLI action. Do not create an HA or network write path; only `control.py` may write the clock.

### HA installation watchdog

- **What it does / why the owner cares:** Produces one actionable “house system health” alert rather than discovering days later that backups, updates, the inverter integration, or core services stopped working.

- **Effort:** Dashboard plus template/command health sensors; a small healthcheck integration may be needed.
- **Risk or catch:** Backup timestamps are currently `unknown`, so the first task is fixing or validating backup integration visibility. “Unknown” must not be treated as healthy.

### Aquarium consumables and maintenance concierge

- **What it does / why the owner cares:** Gives advance, quiet reminders for dosing supplements, calibration, dosing-head/tube replacement, and ReefMat roll changes, while ignoring intentionally unused heads 3 and 4.

### Morning and evening household briefing

- **What it does / why the owner cares:** Delivers a concise briefing covering tomorrow’s solar, battery state, Tesla plan, calendar, shopping list, aquarium deadlines, and unusual system faults; the evening version confirms the next cheap-charge window and tomorrow’s conditions.

### Network and antenna fault triage

- **What it does / why the owner cares:** Distinguishes “router deliberately off” from “network unexpectedly failed,” and reports whether the FRITZ/Tenda path or antenna/logger path is the likely problem.

- **Risk or catch:** Most mesh and antenna sensors are currently `unavailable`; a reliable implementation needs local reachability/ping or integration availability sensors. Existing `automation.turn_off_router` and `automation.turn_on_router` must be treated as intentional state changes, not failures.

### Two-phone presence confidence

### Humidity-aware living-room comfort

- **What it does / why the owner cares:** Suggests or optionally applies sensible LG AC operation when the living room is hot/humid, with quiet hours and an energy-cost summary afterward.

- **Rough value:** Comfort improvement; possibly a few pence/day from avoiding unnecessary runtime.
- **Effort:** Template thresholds and an opt-in automation.
- **Risk or catch:** There is no living-room occupancy sensor, so automatic cooling must be conservative and opt-in. Do not infer AC consumption from hourly flat spots; the sensor is known to update in lumps.
